Colombia's Statutory Health Law (Law 1751 of 2015, article 10) grants the following rights and responsibilities to everyone who receives health services. See also the [institution's charter of rights and responsibilities](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1-EShuKKp778muDvWhJqUZ8UnxLFCylFR/view) (in Spanish).

## Your rights

1. Access health services and technologies that provide comprehensive, timely, high-quality care.
2. Receive emergency care without being asked for any document or prior payment.
3. Full, ongoing and clear communication with the professional treating you.
4. Clear and sufficient information to decide freely about the procedures proposed to you and their risks. No one can force you to receive treatment against your will.
5. Receive health benefits under the conditions and terms set by law.
6. Dignified treatment that respects your beliefs, customs and opinions about procedures.
7. Confidential handling of your medical records, which only third parties authorized by you or by law may see. You may also consult your full records free of charge and get a copy.
8. Quality care throughout your illness from trained, licensed professionals.
9. Timely access to the technologies and medicines you need.
10. Services delivered with hygiene, safety and respect for your privacy.
11. Confidentiality of your health information, except where the law or the authorities allow your family to access it.
12. Know the channels for complaints and suggestions, and receive a written response.
13. Ask for and receive explanations about the cost of services.
14. Respect for your decision to donate or not donate your organs, under the law.
15. Protection from cruel or inhuman treatment and avoidable suffering.
16. Not having administrative burdens that belong to institutions shifted onto you.
17. Having all treatment options explored to overcome your illness.

## Your responsibilities

1. Look after your own health, your family's and your community's.
2. Follow the recommendations of health promotion and prevention programs in a timely way.
3. Act in solidarity when someone's life or health is at risk.
4. Respect the staff who provide and manage health services.
5. Use health services and system resources appropriately and responsibly.
6. Follow the rules of the health system.
7. Act in good faith toward the health system.
8. Provide timely and sufficient information for your care.
9. Contribute to the cost of health care according to your ability to pay.

Failing to meet these responsibilities can never be a reason to deny you care that you need.
