A myocardial perfusion scan is a nuclear medicine test. It shows whether blood reaches all parts of the heart muscle well, first at rest and then when the heart works harder.

It is used to find the cause of chest pain, to see whether the heart's arteries are narrowed, and to follow up on people who have already had a heart attack or a cardiac catheterization.

The radiopharmaceutical is used in a small dose, and your body gets rid of it little by little. That is why there are some simple precautions you should follow at home after the test.

If your order is for the **rest** scan only, the preparation is different: see [myocardial perfusion at rest](/en/patients/exam-preparation/#prep-perfusion-reposo).

If you are pregnant or think you might be, or if you are breastfeeding, let us know before the test is scheduled. Also read our [patient safety recommendations for nuclear medicine](/en/patients/patient-safety/).
