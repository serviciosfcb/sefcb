There is often more than one way to look after your heart: a procedure, a medicine, a change in habits, or watching and waiting with regular check-ups. When the evidence does not point to a single best option, the right choice also depends on what matters to you. That is why it is made **between you and your doctor**.

You have the right to clear information about your condition, your options and the risks of each, and to ask every question you need before you give your informed consent.

## Four steps to decide

1. **Clarify the decision:** what needs to be decided, why, and by when.
2. **Know your options:** ask for each one to be explained, with its benefits, its risks and what would happen if you do nothing.
3. **Think about what matters to you:** for example, recovery time, pain, travel, cost or getting back to work soon.
4. **Check whether you are ready:** before deciding, answer these four questions.
    - Do I know the benefits and risks of each option?
    - Am I clear about which benefits and risks matter most to me?
    - Do I have enough support and advice to decide?
    - Do I feel sure about the best choice for me?

If any answer is “no,” talk to your doctor again before you decide.

## A guide to fill in at home

The [Ottawa Personal Decision Guide](https://decisionaid.ohri.ca/docs/das/OPDG.pdf) (PDF) helps you sort out your options and bring your answers to your appointment. A [Spanish version](https://decisionaid.ohri.ca/docs/das/GADO.pdf) is also available. You can print it and fill it in with your family.

Below you can tick the questions you want to ask your doctor and print your list.

**In an emergency,** such as a heart attack, there is no time to decide calmly: the team will act to protect your life and will explain what is needed to you or your family.
